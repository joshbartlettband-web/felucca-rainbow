# Writing for Josh

Anything written for Josh's projects follows this: the website, READMEs, the installer, captions in videos,
commit messages, PR text and replies in chat. The full version, with examples, is Josh's "Writing in Josh's voice" doc.

## The rule: don't break the spell

Write like Josh telling one person about something Josh made. If a line sounds like a brochure, a press release or a
chatbot, cut it or say the plain thing. The apps are toys and games. The words around them should feel like part of
the same world, not like a machine describing it.

Three questions for every line:

1. Would Josh say it out loud to a parent at a table?
2. Does it tell the reader something they didn't know, with a real name or number?
3. If you delete it, does anything get lost?

## Josh's voice

- Short and plain. Get to the point in the first few words. No warm-up, no sign-off.
- Everyday words: "fresh", "show off", "take a look". Not industry words.
- Honest small hedges are fine: "probably", "a good amount of", "I suppose".
- One picture from the world, not three: a jar, a moonbow, a van.
- Polite, never gushing. A plain "please", no praise.
- American spelling. Real numbers. Credit people from r/MVaveFM1 by username.

## Spell breakers

From Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing". One of these alone proves nothing. A pile of them gives the page away.

- Puffing things up: "a testament to", "vital", "rich", "vibrant", "seamless", "groundbreaking", "with attitude"
- Chatbot words: delve, tapestry, realm, landscape, showcase, foster, enhance, crucial, pivotal, intricate, additionally
- Fancy verbs for "is" and "has": serves as, stands as, boasts, features, offers
- "It's not just X, it's Y"
- Everything in threes
- Every bullet opening with a bold label and a colon
- Slogan headings like "Music theory you can see"
- Brackets inside sentences that carry long lists
- Filler like "Each sound was made for its character"
- Tacked-on -ing clauses: "..., highlighting its importance"
- "It's important to note", "In summary", "Overall"
- An em dash in every paragraph
- In chat: "Great question", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", headers and bullet lists for a short answer

## Before it ships

- Read it out loud once.
- Sentences mostly under 20 words, paragraphs of three sentences or fewer.
- Every claim has a real name or number, or it goes.
- Numbers match the app today (friends, characters, beats, venues).
